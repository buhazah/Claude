Rules the system enforces

No cards, no shadows

Panels are hairline containers with a labelled edge. Grouping is done by rules and alignment, so density stays readable and nothing floats for decoration.

Numbers are monospaced

Every figure, everywhere, tabular. Columns align, digits do not reflow while you read, and the interface reads as an instrument.

Motion is for state changes only

No entrance animation, no parallax, no ambient movement. A screen you open forty times a day should be still when you arrive.

Deliberately absent

No customer portal. No chat surface. No notification centre. No settings screen — configuration is a file. Every one of these would be a thing to maintain before it was a thing to need.

The three flows

Everything else is navigation

1 · Morning triage — under two minutes

Command→ Read the attention list→ Open the worst one→ Act, or set a next action→ Back to Command

2 · After a client call — under three minutes, while it is fresh

Client→ Paste the verbatim→ Tag pain / objection / value→ Set the next action→ Rule of three re-runs

The capture form is on the client page rather than behind a menu, because a form that takes thirty seconds to reach is a form that gets filled in from memory on Friday.

3 · Friday synthesis — thirty minutes, calendared

Intelligence→ What repeated this week→ What contradicts last week→ Effort→ What would I change about the offer

What I would build, and in what order

Three of the five surfaces sit on data the ops tool already produces and can be implemented immediately. Two do not, and building screens against invented data is how a design starts lying about the product. The audit workspace is the highest-value next thing — it is what a paying customer actually buys.
